# 0.8.0

Released 2026-10-10. The compiler and every library move to 0.8.0 together.
Programs, like an AI agent or a script, can now work beside a project's people: a
role made for services, a key to sign in with, and the same rules as everyone.

## Services

- **`define role agent "Agent" in project for services`** makes a role a project's
  programs may hold. With one, the language declares `service`: records a project
  makes in its settings, each named by its title and holding one role made for
  services.
- **A service is a member.** It reads what members read and runs what its role
  allows, in its project, and nothing else: no `by anyone signed in` command, no
  other project, and nothing that changes who has access, whatever a role's record
  is edited to say.
- **Keys.** `service::key` gives a service a key, shown once to copy, kept only as
  a hash, replaced by a new one, and refused at once once `service::revoke` takes
  it away. A key runs commands as `Authorization: Bearer one_…`, says who it is at
  `GET /api/me`, and trades at `POST /api/token` for a Firebase sign-in of the
  service's own, to read views live under the same rules as a person.
- A view can show whether someone is a service: `created_by.service`.
- A project with a role for services deploys with its backend allowed to sign
  their sign-ins as itself.

## Commands

- **`held(phase.worked_by)`** asks whether the person holds one of the roles listed
  on what the command's entity points at, so a phase can say which roles work it:

  ```one
  command issue::claim {
  	require held(phase.worked_by)  "this phase isn't worked by a role you hold"
  	require assignees == none || assignees has me  "someone else has taken it"
  	add me to assignees
  }
  ```
